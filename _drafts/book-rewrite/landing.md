# The AI-Native Company

This is a book about how companies are built now that people and AI agents can work together as a single system.

Much of what gets written about AI in business is about tools: which ones to buy, which tasks they take over, and which companies have "added AI" and which haven't. That work is useful, and I draw on a lot of it, but it is not what this book is about. The question I care about is how a company is put together. Where does its memory live, so that people and agents work from the same knowledge? How does the work move in loops that close quickly, so that what the company learns this week changes what it does next week? And where, inside all of that, does a person still make the call?

Everything in the book starts from one sentence:

> AI-native means people and agents work as one system, and the advantage goes to whoever builds that system first.

*Caption:* People and agents share one memory. The work moves in loops that close quickly, and the marked points are where a person decides.

The diagram is that sentence drawn out. On one side are the people and on the other the agents, and between them sits a single memory that both can read and write. The work moves around the loop: something happens, the company learns from it, and what it learns changes the next round of work. The marked points are the moments where a person, not a model, makes the decision. Each topic in this book takes one part of this picture and looks at it closely, and the Definition topic explains the full working definition behind it.

## Written in public

This book is being written in public, one topic at a time, and that has a consequence worth stating at the start: some of my views will change as I learn. When one does, I won't quietly rewrite the page. Each topic carries a version number and a changelog, and every change is recorded there with its date, so you can see what I thought before and why I changed my mind.

It is the same habit that helped settle an earlier argument. "Cloud-native" also meant whatever a vendor needed it to mean, until a community wrote a definition down and kept maintaining it in the open. This book is one person keeping a set of pages rather than a foundation, but the practice is the same.
